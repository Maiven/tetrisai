# Global Case Map 2026
## What existing products solve — and the gap Bandaepyeon should own

_Last reviewed: 2026-09-20_

This is a product case map, not a claim that these are all companies in the category. The goal is to study representative global products that already solve adjacent pieces of startup career decision-making and convert their strongest patterns into product requirements.

---

## 1. Startup job marketplaces

### Wellfound
https://wellfound.com/candidates/overview  
https://wellfound.com/hiring-data

What it proves:
- Startup candidates value salary and equity before applying.
- Wellfound exposes salary/equity up front.
- It also surfaces hiring-stage and recruiter-response signals.
- Candidate search is private by default.
- More than 27K startups and 10M+ candidates are claimed on the current product page.

Product lesson:
- **Transparency should appear before commitment, not after.**
- Response behavior can be a useful signal.
- Candidate privacy must be default, not premium.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Do not rebuild a job marketplace.
- Start after the candidate has a real decision.
- Route users to compensation and listing evidence when relevant.

## 2. Company storytelling / culture discovery

### Welcome to the Jungle
https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en-GB  
https://help.welcometothejungle.com/en/are-my-applications-visible-to-other-companies

What it proves:
- Candidates want richer company context than a job description.
- Photos, video, team information, culture, and company stories reduce abstraction.
- Candidate application history is private.

Product lesson:
- Company context is part of choice quality.
- Privacy is a product feature.
- Employer-generated content is useful but remains a company-authored source.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Employer claims should be tagged as **documented/company-authored**, not as verified reality.
- Candidate-facing Data Room can be a future B2B2C layer.

## 3. Organizational transparency

### The Org
https://theorg.com/for-candidates  
https://support.theorg.com/en/articles/6797266-jobs-on-the-org

What it proves:
- Candidates care who they will work with, reporting lines, team structure, and career paths.
- Org structure can make role reality more visible before joining.
- The Org explicitly positions transparency as valuable to candidates.

Product lesson:
- "Who decides?" and "Who will I work with?" are not soft culture questions.
- Org structure is evidence for Role Reality and Leadership lenses.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Add **team / reporting-line verification** to the source router.
- Long-term: compare documented role scope with actual org position.

## 4. Employee-review communities

### Glassdoor
https://www.glassdoor.com/about/trust/

### Blind
https://www.teamblind.com/

What they prove:
- Workers seek peer-generated information about culture, management, compensation, interviews, and career growth.
- Anonymity creates access to information companies may not publish.
- Review platforms invest heavily in moderation and trust because anonymous content can be noisy or manipulated.

Product lesson:
- Anonymous reviews are useful **context signals**, not facts about one candidate's future manager or role.
- Multiple sources should be triangulated.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Treat reviews as lower-tier evidence.
- Never turn review sentiment into a deterministic "company risk score."
- Ask: "What specific claim from reviews should I verify with a current employee?"

### Levels.fyi
https://www.levels.fyi/services/

Current adjacent commercial proof:

- Startup Package: $1,250.

### Wellfound salary data
https://wellfound.com/hiring-data

What they prove:
- Compensation benchmarking is valuable enough to attract significant traffic and paid services.
- Startup offers require role/location/stage context.

Product lesson:
- Price is only one part of the decision.
- Users will pay at an actual offer deadline.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Do not compete as a salary database.
- Use external compensation benchmarks as evidence in the **Cash & Equity** lens.
- Monetize the decision moment, not generic monthly chat.

## 6. High-touch negotiation / career coaching

### Rora
https://www.teamrora.com/

What it proves:
- Elite technical talent pays for human support at high-stakes offer moments.
- Rora uses real-time market data and human expertise.
- Pricing can be tied to negotiated improvement.
- A major value proposition is negotiating without damaging relationships.

Product lesson:
- **Social friction** is part of the problem.
- Knowing the question is not enough; candidates need wording that preserves the relationship.
- Human expertise remains valuable for complex cases.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Candidate Diligence Request should be professional and non-accusatory.
- Future: expert escalation / referral for negotiation, tax, legal, or executive-level cases.
- Do not pretend AI replaces human counseling.

## 7. Employee equity planning

### Secfi
https://secfi.com/tools/equity-planner  
https://secfi.com/learn/equity-compensation-planning  
https://secfi.com/for-startups

What it proves:
- Equity is complicated enough to support self-service planning, professional guidance, financing, and employer-sponsored education.
- Users benefit from side-by-side scenarios.
- Equity providers can sell B2B2C through employers while serving employees directly.

Product lesson:
- Equity should be an evidence workflow, not a single "value" field.
- Scenario analysis is valuable.
- Employer-sponsored access can work when employee trust is preserved.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Never model equity value without explicit assumptions.
- Route to official grant documents and jurisdiction-specific guidance.
- Sponsored Access is plausible, but sponsors must not see personal decision content.

## 8. Investor-grade company due diligence

### PitchBook
https://pitchbook.com/use-cases/due-diligence

### Crunchbase
https://www.crunchbase.com/

What they prove:
- Investors pay heavily for financing history, investors, leadership changes, market activity, and alerts.
- Private-company decision-making requires data from multiple sources.

Product lesson:
- Employees are economically smaller buyers but face the same structural problem: **important decisions under private-company information asymmetry**.
- Public evidence should be time-stamped and source-linked.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Public Evidence Agent is a real product layer.
- Long-term: authorized integrations or licensed sources, not scraping.
- Keep "public signal" separate from "internal fact."

### ResumeTurtle
https://www.resumeturtle.com/job-offer-comparison-calculator

### Other comparison calculators
Multiple products compare:
- salary
- bonus
- equity
- benefits
- PTO
- commute / remote costs

What they prove:
- Side-by-side comparison is an intuitive decision UX.
- No-login calculators lower first-use friction.

Product lesson:
- Candidates often have **multiple real options**, not one isolated offer.
- Compensation comparison is already commoditized.

Bandaepyeon implication:
- Future comparison mode should compare **evidence quality and career option quality**, not just money.
- A useful matrix would compare:
  - unresolved material unknowns
  - real authority
  - manager evidence
  - equity evidence
  - 12-month career capital
  - reversibility
- Do not declare a "winner."

# 10. The global whitespace

Existing categories solve fragments:

| Category | What it solves | What remains |
|---|---|---|
| Job marketplace | discovery, salary/equity listing | offer-specific unknowns |
| Employer profile | company story and culture | company-authored bias |
| Org chart | team structure | actual authority / manager behavior |
| Review community | peer context | noisy, non-specific evidence |
| Salary benchmark | market pay | role/company/manager risk |
| Equity planner | equity mechanics | company + role decision |
| Human coach | deep judgment | expensive, limited scale |
| Investor database | company intelligence | not candidate-owned |
| Offer calculator | financial comparison | evidence quality / uncertainty |

The unowned workflow is:

# **Candidate-owned decision due diligence after a real offer exists**

`Offer → Unknowns → Source routing → Questions → Company response → Evidence quality → Decision delta → Expectation memo → 30/90-day calibration`

# 11. Product requirements from global case research

## A. Evidence Router
For every missing claim, show the best verification source:
- accountable company owner
- current employee
- official company document
- regulator / government source
- compensation benchmark
- org chart
- peer review source
- private-market data source

## B. Decision Delta
When new evidence arrives, show:
- which lens changed
- what was missing before
- what became verified
- what remains unresolved
- which flip condition was affected

The Evidence Loop should visibly change the product state.

## C. Comparison without a winner
Support "Offer A vs Offer B vs Stay" but compare evidence and trade-offs rather than scoring a political-style or deterministic winner.

## D. Candidate Data Room / Expectation Memo
Create a portable, candidate-owned record of:
- role expectations
- decision rights
- compensation terms
- unresolved items
- answer provenance
- 30/90-day review

## E. Human escalation
When the issue crosses into:
- tax
- securities law
- employment law
- immigration
- executive negotiation
route to qualified professionals rather than hallucinating.

## F. Source provenance
Every statement should remain distinguishable as:
- company-authored
- regulator / official
- accountable person
- current employee
- third-party media
- anonymous review
- AI inference

# 12. What not to build

Do not become:
- another generic job board
- another review site
- another salary calculator
- another "AI says accept/reject" app
- another employer flight-risk surveillance product

The defensible category is the **workflow between offer and commitment**.

# 13. Global commercial thesis

B2C:
- one high-stakes decision
- 7–14 day diligence window
- price experiment: $9 / $19 / $29

B2B2C:
- recruiting marketplace
- accelerator / VC portfolio benefit
- career community
- employee financial-wellness partner

Long-term B2B:
- candidate-facing transparency / data-room tooling
- only if Personal and employer data remain structurally separated

Core trust rule:

> **The company may answer the candidate. It may never inspect the candidate's private decision room.**
